# Module 10 — Counter-OSINT & Personal OPSEC

## Concept
Everything you've learned to *find*, you should also learn to *hide* about
yourself. This is the module most students skip — and shouldn't.

## Personal Audit Checklist
- [ ] Search your own name/username/email in quotes on Google
- [ ] Check HaveIBeenPwned for your email(s)
- [ ] Run Sherlock on your usernames to see linked accounts
- [ ] Review EXIF data on photos before posting (strip GPS if present)
- [ ] Review Facebook/Instagram "About" section — remove hometown/workplace if
      not needed publicly
- [ ] Check WHOIS privacy status on any domains you own
- [ ] Search old/abandoned accounts (old Twitter, old GitHub) tied to your
      name or email — delete or lock down

## Practical OPSEC Habits
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| GPS metadata in photos | Disable location tagging in camera app settings |
| Reused usernames across sites | Use unique usernames for sensitive accounts |
| Reused passwords | Use a password manager; enable 2FA everywhere |
| Public "check-ins" in real time | Post travel/location content *after* you've left |
| Background details in photos (mail, signage) | Review backgrounds before posting |
| Data broker exposure | Use opt-out tools/services where available in your country |
| Old breached accounts | Rotate passwords immediately when notified of a breach |

## For Public-Facing Professionals (Journalists, Researchers, Creators)
- Separate a "professional" public identity from personal accounts
- Use a business address / PO box instead of a home address where required
- Consider a VPN + separate research browser profile with no personal logins

## Quick Wins (Do These Today)
1. Enable 2FA on email (your account recovery hub)
2. Set social media location tagging to "off" by default
3. Search your name in quotes and note anything surprising
4. Check HaveIBeenPwned and rotate any exposed passwords

## Closing Thought
The best OSINT practitioners understand offense *and* defense — because
understanding how a target's footprint gets built is exactly how you learn to
minimize your own.
